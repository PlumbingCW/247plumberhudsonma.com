Toilet overflowing: stop the water first

Toilet repair in Hudson, MA is an emergency when water is on the floor. Dispatch is open 24/7 and we offer same-day appointments when available. These steps buy you time until we arrive.

  • Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise. It is on the wall or floor near the base.
  • If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and hold the float up to stop the fill.
  • Do not flush again. Wet floors are slippery and sewage carries bacteria.
  • Put towels at the base and keep children and pets away.

Why toilets fail

Most toilet calls come from one of a few parts. A flapper that does not seal lets water leak into the bowl and makes the fill valve run. A worn fill valve will not shut off. A loose or cracked wax ring leaks at the base and stains the ceiling below.

A clog is a different problem from a mechanical failure. If the bowl rises but does not spill, plunge it gently with a flange plunger. If it keeps happening, the trouble is further down the line and needs drain cleaning.

Repair or replace the toilet

Parts are inexpensive, so a toilet with a worn flapper or fill valve is nearly always worth repairing. A cracked bowl cannot be fixed. A toilet that wastes water or needs frequent attention may be worth swapping for a modern low-flow model.

We tell you which it is and what each costs. Many homes in Hudson have older toilets that use more water per flush than current models.

Wax rings, flanges and floor damage

When a toilet leaks at the base, we pull it and inspect the flange, which is the ring the toilet bolts to. A broken or low flange will not hold a seal. We repair it with a new ring and fresh hardware.

We also check the subfloor. A slow leak under a toilet can soften the wood. If it is soft, we tell you before we set the new toilet on it.

Toilets in rentals and commercial spaces

Landlords and managers call us after hours for stopped toilets. Flush valve and tank-style units each have their own parts. See commercial plumbing for flush valve and restroom work.

Tell dispatch whether the unit is a standard tank toilet or a commercial flush valve unit.

Preventing the next overflow

Use only toilet paper and human waste in the bowl. Wipes sold as flushable, paper towels, cotton swabs and feminine products are the most common objects we pull out of a trap or find tangled in a line.

Learn where the shutoff valve is and turn it once a year so it does not seize. Replace a rubber supply hose that is cracked or bulging, since a failed hose floods a bathroom quickly. If you have young children, a lid lock helps, since toys are another common cause of a blocked trap.

What the code says about toilet work

Repairing leaks in valves, and clearing stoppages, are exempt from permits under 248 CMR 3.05. Replacing a toilet or altering its drain is not, and the work must meet the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00.

A modern toilet uses less water per flush than an older one, which is one reason a leaky old unit can be worth replacing.
