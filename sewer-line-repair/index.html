Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewer line repair in Hudson, MA becomes an emergency when wastewater comes back up into the house. Dispatch is open 24/7 and we offer same-day appointments when available. Sewage carries bacteria, so treat any backup as a health issue.

  • Stop using every drain and flush nothing. Water you add will come back up through the lowest fixture.
  • Keep children and pets out of the affected area, and do not walk through standing sewage in bare feet.
  • Shut off power to a flooded basement only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  • Call us, then call your insurer. Sewer backup is often a separate policy rider, so ask what yours says.

What breaks a sewer line

The cause decides the repair. In older Hudson neighborhoods, the lateral from the house to the street can be clay tile, cast iron or Orangeburg fiber pipe. Clay joints let roots in. Cast iron scales and cracks along the bottom. Orangeburg softens and collapses.

Roots enter at joints and spread into a mass that catches paper. Ground movement from frost and from heavy trucks can shift a pipe and create a belly where water pools. A belly holds sewage and debris, and the clog returns again and again.

Hudson's own collection system, run by the DPW, includes 51 miles of gravity sewer, 15 pump stations and 7 miles of force main. The Town investigates inflow and infiltration, which is groundwater leaking into old pipes. Your lateral is a smaller version of the same problem.

How we find the break

We run a camera through the line before we cut anything. The footage shows roots, cracks, offsets, bellies and collapses. We mark the depth and location with a locator so the dig is small.

Many homeowners skip the camera and pay twice. A line that is cleared and not inspected will clog again if a break is the cause. The sewer inspection page explains what you receive.

Cast iron and old clay: what to expect

Cast iron drain stacks inside older homes crack at the bottom of horizontal runs. We can sleeve a short cracked section with a repair coupling, or cut it out and replace it with new pipe. Clay lateral lines are replaced or lined.

Roots will return to a clay joint within a few years unless the joint is sealed or replaced. We tell you what we see so you can decide.

Permits, digging and the Town

Sewer work involves the Town. A licensed plumber pulls a permit through the Hudson Building Department at 78 Main Street, and a connection to the public sewer is inspected. Call 811 before any excavation. The Town says nothing but sewage may be discharged into the sewer, and sump pumps may not discharge into it.

If the camera shows the blockage is in the Town's main and not your lateral, the DPW handles it. We show you the footage so you can give the DPW the location.

Rules for sewer work

Sewer repairs follow the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00 and need a permit under 248 CMR 3.05, with an inspection before the trench is closed. Call 811 before any digging, which is a standard step for excavation in Massachusetts.

The Town of Hudson DPW publishes sewer rules, and a connection to the public sewer must follow them.
