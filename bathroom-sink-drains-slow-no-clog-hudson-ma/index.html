Bathroom Sink Drains Slow With No Clog: A Hudson, MA Homeowner Guide

By the Hudson 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026.

A sink that drains slowly but does not fully back up is giving you a warning. Here are the causes, the checks you can do and when to call.

Call (508) 389-4045

Why a slow drain with no clog is common

People often say there is no clog because the sink still drains, only slowly. A slow drain is a partial blockage. Hair, toothpaste, soap scum and lotion build up on the inside of the pipe and narrow the opening. The pipe is not full yet, but water cannot pass at full speed.

The cause can be right under the stopper, further down in the trap, in the branch line or, less often, in the vent that lets air into the system. Work from the easiest and closest to the hardest. If you want the problem fixed without the steps, see drain cleaning.

How to clean a pop-up stopper

Many bathroom sinks have a pop-up stopper connected to a rod behind the faucet. Look under the sink for a small nut where the rod meets the drain pipe. Loosen it, pull the rod out of the stopper, and lift the stopper out of the sink. A clump of hair and gunk is usually wrapped around the bottom.

Wipe it clean, rinse it and put it back. Reconnect the rod and adjust it so the stopper opens and closes. Then run hot water and see if the drain speeds up. Many homeowners are surprised how often this is all it takes.

Cleaning the trap

The P-trap is the U-shaped pipe under the sink. Put a bucket under it and loosen the two slip nuts. Slide the trap off and empty it. Rinse it out and look at the pipe going into the wall. If it is coated with slime, use a small bottle brush.

Reattach the trap, tighten the slip nuts by hand and run water. Check for drips. If the sink is still slow, the partial blockage is further into the wall. Avoid pouring a chemical drain cleaner into a sink that is already slow. It can sit in the pipe, damage some materials and splash on whoever opens the trap next.

Venting and air

A drain needs air behind the water. If the vent that serves the sink is blocked by a leaf, a nest or ice, the water drains slowly and the trap gurgles. This is more likely if several fixtures on the same line behave the same way, or if the slowdown happens only in cold or windy weather.

A blocked vent is a roof job. We clear it with a line from the roof or from inside. If you have the sign of a vent problem, tell the plumber so the visit starts in the right place.

Who to call if the problem is in the street

According to the Hudson Department of Public Works, only sewage belongs in the Town's sewer collection system, which it maintains. If every drain in the house is slow, or if neighbors have the same problem, the issue may be in the public main, not your pipes. The DPW is at 1 Municipal Drive and can be reached at 978-562-9333.

A camera tells us quickly which side of the property line the problem is on. If it is your lateral, we clear it. If it is the Town main, we show you the footage so the DPW can respond. This saves you paying to clear a line that is not yours.

Hard water and soap scum

Mineral scale and soap scum build up faster in some water. If you have white crust on faucets, the drain pipe inside likely has a similar coating. We do not state a hardness figure for Hudson, and you can ask the Town's water division about the supply you receive. Regular cleaning of the stopper and an occasional flush with hot water slow the buildup.

A drain cleaning that scours the pipe wall, such as jetting or a cable with a scraping head, removes what a plunger cannot. See hydro jetting if the problem returns.

What not to do

Do not pour boiling water into a plastic pipe or onto a porcelain sink, since it can crack or soften them. Do not use a plunger and a chemical together. Do not use a drain stick or a wire that can scratch the pipe and snag in the trap.

Do not ignore a slow drain for months. A drain that is slow today can be fully blocked after a few more weeks of hair and soap. Cleaning early is quicker and cheaper.

A simple monthly habit

Use a hair catcher in the drain or lift the stopper and clean it once a month. Run hot tap water for a minute after brushing teeth or washing up. Keep a small drain brush under the sink to clear the first few inches of the pipe.

This takes two minutes and prevents most slow sink calls. If the sink is already slow, the checks above are the place to start.

Slow drains in rentals and shared bathrooms

Landlords and roommates see slow sinks often, since hair and products build up quickly with several users. A drain strainer and a quick monthly cleaning keep it open. If a tenant reports gurgling or slow drains in several rooms, treat it as a possible main line problem and book a visit before it backs up on a weekend.

When to call a plumber

Call if you have cleaned the stopper and the trap and the sink is still slow, if several fixtures drain slowly, if you hear gurgling or if you smell sewage. Those signs point to a branch or main line problem that needs a machine and sometimes a camera.

We serve homes in Sudbury and Berlin as well. If the sink backs up completely or water comes up elsewhere, treat it as urgent and call dispatch.
