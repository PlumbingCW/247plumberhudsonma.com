Lake Boon belongs to two towns

Lake Boon sits on the Stow and Hudson town line. The Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation describes it as a series of basins linked by narrow channels, with heavily developed shores. Published sources give different acreage figures, so we do not quote one.

This page covers the Hudson side only. Property listings show single-family homes on Lake Boon Drive in Hudson, so that street is a firm anchor. For the Stow shore, see our Stow page. If your address sits close to the line, call and tell dispatch the street.

Seasonal use and year-round use

We do not know how any one lakeside house is used, and we do not guess. Some houses near a lake are lived in all year and some are empty for weeks at a time. The plumbing question is different for each.

A house used only in summer needs its water shut off and drained correctly before cold weather. A house used all year needs working shutoffs and a heater that suits a full household. Ask dispatch about either case.

We check the house, not the neighborhood

We do not assume that lake houses share one type of plumbing or one age. Two neighboring lots can hold very different buildings, so a technician looks at the supply material, the water heater and the drain at your address.

Before any repair, the technician explains what was found and what it means for you. If a part of the system looks near the end of its life, we say so and let you decide.

Cold weather and houses that sit empty

Frozen pipes are a general risk for any house in a Massachusetts winter, and they matter more where a house is empty for days or weeks.

  • Know where the main shutoff is and make sure the valve turns.
  • Keep the heat on at a safe minimum in cold weather.
  • If a pipe has frozen but not split, open the faucet and warm the wall slowly.
  • If a pipe has split, close the main before the pipe thaws and call us.

Water, wells and septic near the lake

The Town of Hudson enforces the Massachusetts septic rules, Title 5, and only licensed installers may work on a septic system. We do not assume which lake homes are on sewer. Ask the Hudson Health Department or DPW, and tell dispatch what you learn.

Work near the shoreline can fall under wetlands rules, so ask the Hudson Conservation Commission before outdoor excavation. The Lake Boon Commission, created in 1941, regulates activity on and around the lake, not the plumbing inside your house.

Before we arrive

A few minutes of preparation limit damage while the technician is on the way.

Shut off the water

Find the main valve where the line enters the house and turn it clockwise. If you cannot find it, close the nearest fixture valve and tell dispatch.

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Never step into standing water near outlets.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone. Open a path to the water heater, the cleanout and the main shutoff.

Call and describe it

Call (508) 389-4045 and tell dispatch your Lake Boon address, where the water is coming from and whether sewage is involved.

Services for lake-area homes

The jobs we match to this area are water heater repair, burst pipe repair after a freeze, and leak detection when a house has been empty. A house with a damp basement may also need a look at its sump pump.
