About Hudson 24/7 Plumbing

A Hudson, MA plumbing service built around one idea: when water is where it should not be, someone should pick up the phone at any hour.

Call (508) 389-4045

What we do

Hudson 24/7 Plumbing is a dispatch-led plumbing service for Hudson, Massachusetts and the towns around it. The phone line is staffed around the clock, and technicians respond to burst pipes, sewer backups, failed water heaters and the other problems that cannot wait. We also schedule planned work, such as repiping, installations and water heater installs.

We work from 2 Coolidge St in Hudson, so the towns closest to us are the ones we reach fastest. The full list is on the service area page.

How we work

These are the practices we hold ourselves to.

  • Stop the damage first, then find the cause. A technician who starts by pricing a repair has skipped the diagnosis.
  • Give a clear price before work starts, and explain the options in plain language.
  • Pull permits through the Hudson Building Department when the work needs them, and have a licensed plumber do that work.
  • Call 811 before digging, and say plainly when a job belongs to someone else, such as a septic hauler or the Town DPW.

What we do not claim

We do not publish customer review counts, star ratings, years in business or awards on this site, because we do not want a number here that you cannot check. Where we say we are licensed and insured, we mean it in the ordinary sense, and we are glad to answer questions about it on the phone.

We also do not quote a fixed price from a description alone. Pipe age, access and what is behind a wall change a job, so we look first. Our service pages list the factors that move each price, so you can see why.

Local rules we work within

The Town of Hudson Department of Public Works runs the public water and sewer systems. The Building Department at 78 Main Street handles plumbing and gas permits. The Massachusetts plumbing and gas code, 248 CMR, says permits are required before plumbing or gas work is installed, replaced or repaired, with exceptions for things such as repairing leaks in faucets and clearing stoppages.

Sump pumps may not discharge into the Hudson sanitary sewer, and only sewage belongs in the sewer at all. We design every job to meet that standard, because a repair that breaks the rules does not last. See sump pump service and sewer line repair for how that shapes the work.

How a typical emergency call goes

A person answers the phone and asks for the address, the problem and whether water is still moving. While the technician is on the way, dispatch talks you through the first steps, which usually means closing the main shutoff, clearing the area and keeping people away from standing water or sewage.

The technician stops the damage first, then finds the cause with the right tools, such as a pipe camera, listening gear or a pressure gauge. You are told what was found and what your options are, and you see the price before any repair begins.

If the job needs a permit, a licensed plumber pulls it through the Hudson Building Department. If the cause belongs to someone else, such as the public main or a septic tank, we say so and point you to the right party.

The services we are asked for most

Most calls fall into a few families. Drains and sewers lead the list, with drain cleaning, main line clearing and hydro jetting. Water heaters come next, whether that is repair or replacement.

Hidden or sudden water comes third, from leak detection to burst pipe repair. Planned work, such as a bathroom rough-in or a repipe, fills the rest of the schedule.

Where we work

We are based in Hudson and serve Marlborough, Stow, Sudbury, Maynard and Berlin. Each town has a page that explains its housing, its utilities and the problems we see there. We can often reach neighboring towns when the schedule allows.

Local knowledge helps. Older Hudson and Maynard houses are more likely to have galvanized pipe and clay laterals. Stow and Berlin homes are more likely to be on wells and septic. Sudbury homes are larger, with more pipe to go wrong.
