Mill-town houses and the pipes inside them

Emergency plumbing in Maynard, MA often means a house built for mill workers more than a century ago. Maynard grew around the Assabet Woolen Mill, which Amory Maynard and William Knight built in 1846 and 1847, and the American Woolen Company later bought and expanded it. The company built its Presidential district between 1902 and 1903, with 206 houses.

Houses that age can still be sound, but their original plumbing has usually been changed in pieces. We see galvanized steel, copper added in the mid-twentieth century and plastic from later repairs, sometimes in the same wall.

Before we arrive

A few steps limit damage until the technician reaches you.

Shut off the water

Find the main valve where the line enters the house and turn it clockwise. If you cannot find it, close the nearest fixture valve and tell dispatch.

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Never step into standing water near outlets.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone. Open a path to the water heater, the cleanout and the main shutoff.

Call and describe it

Call (508) 389-4045 and tell dispatch your Maynard address, where the water is coming from and whether sewage is involved.

The Assabet River and the lowest floors

According to the town's history, the Assabet River flows through Maynard from west to east and drops about thirty feet through the town, which once powered the mills. Buildings near the river and on the lower streets can have basements that sit close to groundwater.

For a plumber, that means two things. First, a basement floor drain that backs up after heavy rain may be a groundwater and sewer problem, not just a clog. Second, a sump pump in these basements works hard, so a backup is worth having. See sump pump repair.

Mill & Main Place and commercial plumbing

The old mill complex, now called Mill & Main Place, houses offices and light industry. Large older commercial buildings have long drain runs, many floor drains and older supply risers. Tenants and managers call after hours when a drain or a water heater fails.

If you manage a space in Maynard, tell dispatch which suite and who can authorize work. See commercial plumbing for what we handle.

Winter in the old houses

Many older houses have bathrooms on exterior walls and kitchens over unheated space. Pipes in those walls freeze in the coldest weeks. Keep cabinet doors open, let a faucet drip and know where the shutoff is.

If a pipe splits, shut the main and call. See burst pipe repair.

What a visit looks like

When you call, dispatch asks where the water is, whether it is still running and whether sewage is involved. We give an arrival window and can talk you through the shutoff over the phone. In a tight older house, we protect floors and stairs before we bring in equipment.

The technician stops the water, finds the cause and shows you the options before any repair. Pricing is given first. If the fix needs a permit, we handle it through the town, and we label the shutoffs we find so the next repair is faster.
