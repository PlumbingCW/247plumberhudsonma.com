Start here: wells, septic and what that means for a plumber

Emergency plumbing in Stow, MA often begins with a question dispatch asks that you may not hear in a city: are you on a private well and a septic system? Many Stow homes are, and the answer changes the cause and the fix. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and Stow is a direct neighbor to the north of Hudson, about five miles away.

A drain that backs up on town sewer is a pipe problem. The same symptom on septic can mean a full tank, a blocked outlet or a failed leach field, and a plumber cannot solve that with a cable alone.

Before we arrive

These steps limit damage until the technician reaches you.

Shut off the water

Find the main valve where the line enters the house and turn it clockwise. If you cannot find it, close the nearest fixture valve and tell dispatch.

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Never step into standing water near outlets.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone. Open a path to the water heater, the cleanout and the main shutoff.

Private wells and the Town

According to the Town, the Stow Board of Health has adopted private well regulations, most recently revised in February 2024. They govern how new wells are built and tested, and they are worth reading if you are replacing a well or buying a house with one.

For a plumbing call, the practical point is that a well supply behaves differently from city water. A pump cycling on and off often points to a waterlogged pressure tank. Rust or sediment after a pump replacement is common for a short time. If water changes color or smell, have it tested before you assume the plumbing is at fault.

Fall and winter checklist for country plumbing

Before freezing weather, shut and drain outdoor spigots, insulate pipes in garages and crawlspaces and check that the well pump house or enclosure keeps its heat source. Know where the main shutoff and the pressure tank valve are.

A frozen pipe that splits will flood the space when it thaws. Close the main and call right away. Ask us about replacing old shutoff valves and frost-proof spigots in the fall.
