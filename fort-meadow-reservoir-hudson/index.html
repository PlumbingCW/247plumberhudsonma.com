The Hudson side of the reservoir

Fort Meadow Reservoir lies between Hudson and Marlborough. Reference sources give it a surface area of about 260 acres and a length of roughly one and a half miles. Centennial Beach, on the Hudson side, is run by the Town of Hudson through its Park Commission and Division of Recreation.

This page is for Hudson addresses near that shore, including homes on Fort Meadow Drive. We do not draw a boundary around the area, because the roads that count as the reservoir area vary with who you ask. If you are not sure whether your address fits, call and tell dispatch the street.

How the reservoir is managed

Hudson has a Fort Meadow Commission, set up under Chapter 487 of the Acts of 1953, with a member appointed by the Board of Selectmen. The commission has the power to make reasonable rules after a public hearing. It governs activity on the water, not the plumbing inside your house.

That distinction helps when a job runs close to the shore. Work outdoors near a water body can fall under wetlands rules. Before any outdoor excavation, such as a new water line or a drain trench, ask the Hudson Conservation Commission whether it needs review.

Plumbing questions that come with a lakeside address

We do not assume a lakeside house has a particular problem. We look at what is actually installed. These are the questions we start with.

  • Is the house on town sewer or on a septic system? The answer changes what can go down a drain.
  • Does the basement have a sump pump, and where does it discharge?
  • Is the house used all year, or does it sit empty for part of the year?
  • How old is the water heater, and where is the shutoff for the whole house?

Sump pumps, drains and where the water goes

The Town of Hudson DPW says a sump pump connected to the sanitary sewer is illegal. Roof drains, downspouts and area drains that connect to the sewer should be redirected to a dry well, a landscaped area or rain barrels. A connection to the storm drain is allowed only with permission from the DPW.

If you live near the water and your basement gets damp, check that the pump runs and that its discharge goes outdoors. Our sump pump page explains what a replacement involves.

Septic or sewer: ask before you assume

The Town of Hudson enforces the Massachusetts septic rules, Title 5, and says only licensed installers may work on a septic system. The Town page does not say which streets are on sewer. If you are not sure, your Health Department or DPW can tell you.

We handle the plumbing side, including drains and fixtures, and we tell you plainly when a problem belongs to a septic specialist. A slow drain on a septic house may be a full tank and not a pipe problem.

Before we arrive

A few minutes of preparation limit damage while the technician is on the way.

Shut off the water

Find the main valve where the line enters the house and turn it clockwise. If you cannot find it, close the nearest fixture valve and tell dispatch.

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Never step into standing water near outlets.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone. Open a path to the water heater, the cleanout and the main shutoff.

Call and describe it

Call (508) 389-4045 and tell dispatch your Fort Meadow address, where the water is coming from and whether sewage is involved.
