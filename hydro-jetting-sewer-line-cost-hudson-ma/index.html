Hydro Jetting Sewer Line Cost for Hudson, MA Homeowners

By the Hudson 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026.

You will not find an honest flat price for jetting, because the job depends on the line. Here is what moves the number and how to judge a quote.

Call (508) 389-4045

The honest answer on cost

Hydro jetting cost depends on a few facts about your line, and any plumber who quotes a firm number over the phone without them is guessing. We do not publish a price list. We give you a written price after we see the cleanout and, ideally, the camera footage. This page explains what drives the price so you can compare quotes fairly.

If your drain is backed up right now, skip to our emergency jetting page and call. If you are planning ahead, read on.

Jetting versus snaking on price

A cable machine costs less than jetting, and for a simple clog that is the right tool. Jetting costs more because the equipment is heavier and the job takes longer, but it cleans the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the middle. A line that clogs again soon after snaking is often cheaper to jet once than to snake three times.

The way to judge is to ask what the plumber expects to find. If a camera shows scale and grease, jetting is a good fit. If it shows a broken pipe, jetting is the wrong tool and you should pay for repair instead. See sewer line repair.

Camera first, always

A camera look before jetting is part of a safe job. It shows whether the pipe is sound enough to take high pressure water. Jetting a cracked or collapsed pipe can make it worse and can push debris into the soil. A plumber who offers to jet without looking first is taking a risk with your pipe.

The camera adds to the price, but it also saves you from paying for jetting that does not help. We include the footage and a plain description. The sewer inspection page explains the report.

What the Town system looks like from your pipe

Hudson's sewer collection system is run by the Department of Public Works. It includes about 51 miles of gravity sewer, 15 pump stations and 7 miles of force main. The Town investigates inflow and infiltration, which is groundwater and stormwater leaking into old pipes, and it publishes sewer rules that say only sewage may be discharged to the sewer.

That matters for cost in a practical way. Grease and wipes that leave your house reach a pump station and can block it. A line that is jetted clean stays clear longer if the household keeps grease out. If you are unsure whether a blockage is in your lateral or the Town main, call the DPW at 978-562-9333. A camera tells us which side the problem is on before you pay for work on the wrong one.

When jetting does not make sense

Skip jetting if the camera shows a collapsed pipe, a major offset or very old Orangeburg pipe, which can come apart under pressure. In those cases the money is better spent on repair or lining. Jetting also does not seal a root entry at a joint, so roots will come back unless the joint is fixed.

If your drain is slow but not blocked, ask whether a snake or a smaller cleaning would do. We would rather give you the right fix than the biggest one.

How to compare quotes

Ask each plumber what is included. Does the price cover a camera look before and after? Is it a one pass price or does it allow additional passes? Who pays for a toilet pull and reset? What happens if the line is damaged? A quote that is vague about these is hard to compare.

Get the price in writing before work begins. If the technician finds something new, ask for a revised price before they proceed. Customers in Sudbury and Maynard ask the same questions, and we answer them on the first call.

Questions to ask before you approve a job

Ask whether the price covers a camera before and after, how many passes are included and what happens if the pipe is damaged. Ask who handles a toilet pull and reset. Ask what proof you will receive that the line is clean. A written scope that answers these is easy to compare.

Ask also what the plan is if the camera finds a broken pipe. A good plumber stops, shows you the footage and gives a separate price for the repair. You decide before more money is spent.

How often jetting is worth repeating

A line that has been jetted and is in sound condition may stay clear for years if grease and wipes stay out of it. A line with roots at a joint will need another cleaning in a year or two unless the joint is repaired. A restaurant line may need regular jetting on a schedule.

We suggest a camera look after the first jetting, and again a year later if the line has a history of problems. That tells you whether to plan a repair or keep cleaning.

Seasonal timing

Frozen ground makes outside work harder, but jetting through a cleanout is an indoor job and runs all year. Roots are most active in the growing season, so a spring cleaning often catches them early. If your line backs up in winter, do not wait for spring. Call and we plan around the weather.

Insurance and who pays

Standard homeowners insurance rarely covers cleaning a line that clogged over time. A sewer backup rider may cover damage inside the house after a backup, but the cleaning itself is usually your expense. Read the policy or call your agent before you assume.

If a backup has already damaged the basement, document it with photos before cleanup and keep receipts. Tell your agent early. The cost of jetting is small compared with the cost of finished basement flooring, and the documentation helps with any claim.
