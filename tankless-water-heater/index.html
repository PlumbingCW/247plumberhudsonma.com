Tankless units that stop heating

Tankless water heater service in Hudson, MA starts with the symptom. A unit that shows an error code, delivers lukewarm water or cycles on and off usually has a scale, flow or venting issue. Dispatch is open 24/7 for a unit that has stopped producing hot water entirely.

Tell us the brand, the model if you can read it and the code on the display. That lets the technician bring the right parts.

How a tankless heater works

A tankless unit heats water as it flows through a heat exchanger. A flow sensor starts the burner or the elements when water moves. When flow drops too low, the unit may shut off. When flow is too high, it cannot keep up and the water runs cooler.

This is why a tankless heater can seem fine in one bathroom and fail when two fixtures run at once.

Descaling and flushing

Mineral scale builds up inside a tankless heat exchanger and reduces its output. A flush circulates a mild descaling solution through the unit with a small pump. Manufacturers generally recommend a flush on a regular schedule, and the interval depends on the water.

We do not state a hardness figure for Hudson. Water quality varies by supply, and you can ask the Town water division about yours. If you see scale on faucets or kettles, plan on regular flushes.

Venting, gas and electrical needs

Tankless gas units need a gas line sized for the higher burner input and a vent made of the material the manufacturer lists. Many homes with a tank have a gas pipe that is too small. Electric tankless units draw a lot of current and need heavy-gauge wire and a large breaker.

Those are reasons a conversion costs more than a tank swap. We confirm both before quoting.

Is tankless right for your house?

Tankless units save space and do not run out of hot water on a long shower. They cost more to install, they need maintenance and they do not suit every plumbing layout. A tank may be a better fit for a small household with an existing vent and no space for new gas piping.

We tell you which side we think fits your home and what each option costs. See water heater installation for tank options.

Maintenance that prevents callouts

Three habits keep a tankless unit running. Clean the inlet filter when the unit is flushed. Keep the area around the unit and the vent terminal clear of snow, leaves and storage. Run a flush on the schedule in the manual.

In a cold climate such as ours, a unit on an exterior wall or in an unheated space needs freeze protection. Most models include a heater strip, but a power loss in a cold snap can still leave water standing in the exchanger. If your unit sits somewhere cold, ask us about it.

Venting standards for tankless units

Gas tankless units must be installed to the manufacturer's instructions and to the Massachusetts Fuel Gas Code, 248 CMR 4.00. The state fuel gas code builds on NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, with Massachusetts amendments.

Vent materials and lengths come from the manual for your model, and the inspector checks them. A permit is required before gas work is installed or altered.
