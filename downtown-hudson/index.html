Where Downtown Hudson sits

Downtown Hudson is the area around Main Street, which carries Route 62 through the center of town, close to the Assabet River. A Massachusetts Historical Commission survey of Hudson names Feltonville, now Hudson Center, as the early mill site at Assabet Falls, where the town had its only significant waterpower.

That survey dates from 1980, so we use it for history and not for today's conditions. For plumbing the useful point is simple. The center of Hudson grew in stages, so the buildings come from many periods, and the plumbing in each one has to be checked on site instead of assumed.

Storefronts, apartments and houses within a few blocks

The same survey describes Main Street blocks from different eras. Early ones were two-story wood-frame buildings in Second Empire and Italianate styles, and heavy development in the late 1880s and 1890s added three and four story brick blocks. It also notes larger, more elaborate houses around Lincoln Street, simpler workers' cottages east of the center near Broad and Pleasant Streets, and modest two-story houses of about 1900 to 1915 south of Packard Street.

In practice a call from this part of town can come from a restaurant, a shop, a rented unit or a single-family house. Each one needs a different first question. A blockage in a building with several units may sit in a shared drain, so the owner or manager often needs to be told before a plumber opens anything.

Hudson rules that matter in the center of town

The Town of Hudson has a grease interceptor regulation, No. 08-2, that covers food service facilities. It says new and expanded facilities need an interceptor, requires monthly inspection and cleaning before grease passes 25 percent of capacity and requires written logs kept for at least two years. A restaurant owner should read it before hiring anyone to work on a kitchen drain.

The Town DPW also says a sump pump connected to the sanitary sewer is illegal. If a downtown basement has a sump, the discharge belongs outdoors in an approved place. See our commercial plumbing page for food service work and our sump pump page for basement pumps.

Before we arrive

A few minutes of preparation limit damage while the technician is on the way.

Shut off the water

Find the main valve where the line enters the house and turn it clockwise. If you cannot find it, close the nearest fixture valve and tell dispatch.

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Never step into standing water near outlets.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone. Open a path to the water heater, the cleanout and the main shutoff.
