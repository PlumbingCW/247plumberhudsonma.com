Installing a Gas Hot Water Heater in Hudson, MA

By the Hudson 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026.

A gas water heater swap looks simple and has more ways to go wrong than any other plumbing job. Here is what a safe install includes.

Call (508) 389-4045

Why gas water heaters are different

An electric water heater is a tank, two connections and a power circuit. A gas water heater adds a gas line, a burner, a vent and a source of combustion air. Each of those can fail in a way that harms people, which is why the code treats the job as regulated work and why we do not recommend it as a DIY project.

If you are replacing a failed unit, our water heater replacement service handles the whole process. If you are planning ahead, the sections below explain what a proper job includes.

What the install includes

A safe installation checks each of these parts.

  • A new unit sized to the first hour rating that matches your household.
  • A gas shutoff valve, a sediment trap and a flexible gas connector in good condition.
  • A vent that slopes up, is sealed at each joint and ends at the right height above the roof or wall.
  • Combustion air from a room large enough or from a duct to outside.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve piped to a safe drain location.
  • A drain pan with a drain line where a leak would damage a floor.
  • An expansion tank where the house plumbing is a closed system.

Gas leaks and what to do

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 from outside. Do not turn on lights or use a phone inside. Do not try to find the leak yourself. After the fire department or the gas utility clears the building, we can check the water heater connections.

A properly installed gas connection is leak tested with a pressure gauge or a leak detecting solution before the unit is lit. That test is part of every install.

Permits, inspection and emergency replacements

According to the Massachusetts plumbing and gas code, 248 CMR, a permit is required before plumbing or gas work is installed, replaced or repaired. The permit is pulled by the licensed plumber or gas fitter, and the inspector can require testing and a final inspection. A permit becomes invalid if work does not start within 90 days.

The code also makes room for real emergencies. A fixture or gas appliance may be installed or turned on temporarily before the permit if the licensee has tested it, is satisfied it will operate safely and notifies the inspector. A regular inspection must follow no later than the next working day. In Hudson, the contact is the Building Department at 78 Main Street.

Sizing, fuel choice and the cost of conversion

A forty gallon tank suits many two or three person households, and a fifty gallon tank suits larger families. A larger tank costs more and loses more heat. If you are thinking about switching from gas to electric or to a heat pump, the electrical capacity of the panel decides the cost.

See tankless options if you want on-demand heat. A conversion changes venting and gas sizing and should be priced as a separate project.

Maintenance that extends the life of a gas heater

Drain a quart of water from the tank valve every few months to flush sediment. Test the temperature and pressure relief valve once a year by lifting its lever briefly, and have it replaced if it leaks afterward. Check the anode rod every few years, since it protects the tank from rust.

Keep the area around the heater clear. Paint thinner, gasoline and cleaning supplies stored nearby can create a fire risk. Make sure the vent connections remain tight and the flame is steady and blue.

A tank that is more than ten years old and has begun to rumble, leak or run out of hot water is near the end of its life. Replacing it on your schedule is cheaper than doing it in an emergency.

Signs the heater is unsafe

A yellow or orange flame, soot around the burner or vent and a pilot that keeps going out can all point to poor combustion. A rotten egg smell is gas, and you should leave the house. Rust streaks on the tank or a puddle at the base mean the tank is failing.

If you see any of these, turn the gas control to off if you can reach it safely and call us. For water on the floor, shut the cold supply valve at the top of the heater. Our dispatch line is open 24/7 for failures, and the water heater repair page explains what happens on the call.

Do not try to relight a pilot repeatedly. If it will not stay lit after a try or two, a thermocouple or gas valve may have failed.

What to expect on installation day

The plumber shuts off the gas and water, drains the old tank and disconnects the vent. The old unit is removed and the area is cleaned. The new tank is set in the pan, connected to water with new flex connectors and to gas with a new connector and a sediment trap. The vent is fitted and sealed.

After filling the tank and bleeding the air, the plumber tests every gas joint, lights the burner and checks the flame, the draft and the relief valve. The inspector reviews the work. Expect the house to be without hot water for several hours.

What to ask a plumber before you hire

Ask who pulls the permit, whether the old unit is removed, what the warranty covers and what the vent plan is. A written scope should list the model, the venting, the gas fittings and the price. A quote that does not mention a permit is a warning sign.
