Faucet and Fixture Repair in Hudson, MA

A drip is a leak you pay for. We repair or replace faucets, shower valves, tub spouts and the small shutoff valves under your sinks.

Fixture problems we fix

Faucet repair in Hudson, MA covers kitchen and bathroom faucets, tub and shower valves, outdoor spigots and the supply stops under every sink. A leak that sprays or will not shut off is urgent, and dispatch is open 24/7. A slow drip can wait for a scheduled visit.

If water is coming from the wall or ceiling, skip this page and go to burst pipe repair.

Why faucets drip

Inside a modern faucet is a cartridge or a set of rubber seats and washers. When they wear, water bypasses the seal. Mineral buildup can also scratch the seat. The fix is usually a new cartridge, not a new faucet.

If the faucet is old and the finish is worn, replacement may cost about the same as a repair. We tell you which makes sense.

Shower valves and tub faucets

A shower valve sits inside the wall. Replacing the cartridge needs the trim removed and the handle off, and sometimes the water shut off at the house. If the valve body itself is corroded, the wall must be opened.

A shower that drips after the handle is closed is wasting hot water every minute. It also stains the tub.

Stuck and leaking shutoff valves

The small valves under sinks and behind toilets sit unused for years and then fail when you need them. A stop that will not close means you must shut the whole house to fix a faucet. We replace them with quarter-turn valves that are easier to operate.

Know where your main shutoff is. It is usually in the basement near the water meter. If it will not turn, that valve is worth replacing too.

Faucets and cold weather

An outdoor spigot that is not drained before winter can freeze and split inside the wall. The leak appears in spring when the spigot is turned on. Shut the interior valve for the spigot in fall and open the outdoor one to drain.

If you see water inside the wall, see leak detection to find the source.

Hard water, aerators and low flow

If a faucet flows weakly, the aerator at the tip is the first suspect. Unscrew it, rinse out the grit and soak it in vinegar. If the flow stays weak, the cartridge or the supply line may be restricted.

Low flow at only the hot side points to the water heater, a partly closed stop or a clogged dip tube. Low flow at every fixture in the house points to a larger issue, possibly a failing pressure regulator or a corroded supply line. Do not assume the faucet is the culprit until you have checked those.

When we replace a faucet we also check the supply lines under the sink, because rubber and braided hoses wear out and a failure there sends water straight into the cabinet. We suggest replacing both at the same time, since the cost of the second hose is small once the first is off.

What the code says about fixture work

Repairing faucet leaks is exempt from permits under 248 CMR 3.05. Replacing fixtures or changing supply piping is not exempt, and it must meet the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00.

If a replacement changes the supply piping, we tell you before work starts whether it needs a permit.
