Tankless Water Heater Efficiency for Hudson, MA Homes

By the Hudson 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026 · Updated November 14, 2026.

Tankless heaters waste less energy sitting idle, but the savings depend on how your household uses hot water. Here is how to figure out whether it pays.

Call (508) 389-4045

What "efficient" means for a water heater

Water heater efficiency is measured with a uniform energy factor, or UEF, printed on the label. A higher number means more of the energy you buy ends up as hot water. A standard tank loses heat through its walls all day, which is called standby loss. A tankless unit heats water only when it flows, so it avoids that loss.

That is the reason tankless units are often called efficient. The real question is how much of the loss you will save in your own house, and whether it is enough to cover the higher price and the maintenance. See our tankless service page for the repair side.

Where the savings come from, and where they do not

Households that use a modest amount of hot water spread through the day save more than those that run many showers back to back.

Gas, electric and heat pump

A gas tankless unit often has the best fit for a larger household with gas service, because the burner heats water fast. An electric tankless unit is simple to vent but draws a large amount of current, which may require a new circuit and a panel upgrade. A heat pump tank is the efficiency leader among tanks and is often more efficient than either type of tankless unit in the right setting.

Do not choose on the efficiency number alone. Gas lines, venting and electrical capacity can add cost that eats the savings. We check all three before we quote. See water heater installation for tank options.

What affects real-world efficiency

Short bursts of hot water, such as washing hands, can fall into a cold water sandwich where the water goes hot, cold and hot again. Long pipe runs between the unit and the fixture waste heat and water as you wait. Scale on the heat exchanger reduces output over time, so a unit that is not flushed becomes less efficient and eventually fails.

Setting the temperature no higher than you need saves energy for both types. Insulating hot water pipes cuts loss in a tank house and a tankless house alike.

Hudson has its own electric utility

Hudson Light and Power is the Town's municipal electric department. Its website says it offers energy audits and several rebate programs, and it points customers to an external program page to schedule an audit and apply. It lists a phone number of (978) 568-8736 for questions.

For a water heater decision, that matters in a practical way. Ask the department which rebates apply to electric and heat pump water heaters before you buy, since eligibility and amounts change. We do not state current rebate amounts here because they are not fixed. Check with the utility first.

Maintenance that protects the efficiency

Flush the unit on the manufacturer's schedule. Clean the inlet screen. Keep the vent terminal clear. Water quality varies, and hard water shortens the interval between flushes. If you have scale on faucets, plan on a flush each year.

A neglected tankless unit can cost more in repairs than a tank would. That is the honest trade-off. A flush is quick and inexpensive compared with a replaced heat exchanger.

How to estimate your own savings

Start with your current heater. Check its age, the fuel and the label for its energy factor. Then think about your hot water habits. Count showers, baths, dishwasher cycles and laundry loads on an average day. A household that uses hot water in a few concentrated blocks has more standby loss to save than one that uses it constantly.

Ask for a written scope from at least one installer and compare the upfront cost with the expected savings. A good installer will tell you if the payback period is long. If someone tells you a tankless heater will pay for itself in a few months, ask how they reached that figure.

We do not publish a savings number because it is different in every house. We can walk through your numbers with you before you commit.

Why flow rate matters more than the efficiency number

A tankless heater has a maximum flow rate at a given temperature rise. In cold months, the incoming water is colder, so the unit can heat less water per minute. A model that delivers enough for two showers in July may struggle in January. In a cold climate, size the unit for winter, not summer.

If two showers and a dishwasher run at once, you may need a larger unit or two units. Some homes do better with a smaller tank plus a point of use heater for a distant bathroom. This is a question for the installer to answer with your fixture list in hand.

Low flow fixtures can also trip the minimum flow, so the unit does not fire. If a faucet runs hot and cold, ask about an aerator or a fixture change.

Questions to ask an installer

Ask which unit they recommend and why. Ask what the unit's uniform energy factor is and what the winter flow rate will be with your incoming water temperature. Ask what upgrades the house needs, such as a larger gas line, a new vent or a bigger electrical circuit, and ask for those in writing as separate line items.

Ask about the maintenance plan. A good installer will tell you how often to flush the unit and whether they offer that service. If the answer is that tankless units need no maintenance, find another installer.

When a tank is the better choice

A tank is often the better fit for a small household with an existing vent. It also wins when the current tank is only a few years old, or when a new gas line or electrical circuit would cost more than the savings. A heat pump tank is worth pricing if you have the space.
