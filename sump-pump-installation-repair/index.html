Sump pump not working: first steps

Sump pump repair in Hudson, MA is urgent when water is rising in the pit or the basement. Dispatch is open 24/7 and we offer same-day appointments when available. These steps help while you wait.

  • Check that the pump is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped.
  • Lift the float by hand. If the pump starts, the float is stuck or failed.
  • Do not step into standing water if outlets or equipment are submerged. Shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.
  • Move boxes, furnishings and electronics above the water line.

Why pumps fail

The usual causes are a stuck float, a clogged intake, a burnt motor, a failed check valve or a power outage during a storm. Pumps also wear out. A pump that has run for many years and has a rusty housing is overdue.

A pump that runs constantly may be undersized, or its check valve may be failing and letting water drain back into the pit.

Where the water goes

In Hudson, a sump pump may not discharge into the sanitary sewer. The Town says only sewage belongs in the sewer, and clear water from sump pumps overloads the system. The discharge line should run to the yard, away from the foundation and neighbors.

Do not point the discharge at a neighbor's property or at the street in winter, where it can freeze into ice. We route the pipe to a place that drains.

Battery backups and water-powered backups

A battery backup pump takes over when the main pump fails or the power goes out. It needs a battery that is charged and replaced on schedule. Water-powered backups use house water pressure and need no battery, but they depend on a public supply and use water while running.

Test any backup twice a year by pouring water into the pit.

Preventing basement water

A pump is the last line. Gutters that dump near the foundation and soil that slopes toward the house add to the load. Clean gutters, extend downspouts and check grading.

If water enters through a floor crack or wall joint without a pit, the fix may be drainage or waterproofing, not a pump. We tell you when the job is beyond plumbing and a waterproofing contractor is the right call. A pipe leak behind the wall is a different problem. See leak detection.

Testing and maintenance schedule

Before spring and again in fall, pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the cycle. The float should lift, the pump should start and the water should drop. Listen for grinding or a pump that runs too long. Check that the discharge pipe is clear and the outlet is not buried under leaves.

Every year or two, pull the pump and clean the intake screen. Remove sediment from the pit. If you have a battery backup, test it and check the date on the battery.

Keep a written note of the install date. A pump that has served for many years can fail without warning, and replacing it on your schedule beats replacing it in a storm.

The standards behind a sump pump install

A sump pump discharge must not enter the sanitary sewer, according to the Hudson DPW's sump pump guidance. The discharge piping must meet the standard in the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00 for materials and support.

Wiring for the pump follows the National Electrical Code, NFPA 70. Ask an electrician if you are unsure about the outlet in your basement.
