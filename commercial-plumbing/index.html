When a plumbing problem stops the business

Commercial plumbing in Hudson, MA is the work that keeps restaurants, shops, offices and light industrial buildings open. Dispatch is available 24/7 and we offer same-day appointments when available. Tell us if customers are on site.

  • Shut off the nearest fixture valve or the building main if water is spreading.

What commercial calls look like

The problems resemble residential ones, but the stakes and the pipe sizes are bigger. A restaurant loses its kitchen when a floor drain backs up. A retail shop loses a day when a toilet overflows through a ceiling. An office loses a floor when a supply line fails.

Hudson's downtown has older mixed-use buildings along Main Street, and the Town also has industrial and commercial areas. Older buildings often have mixed pipe materials and several additions, which means shutoffs are not where you expect them.

Grease traps and kitchen drains

Restaurants and food service businesses depend on a grease trap or interceptor. When it is full, grease passes into the line and clogs it. A trap needs regular pumping, and the schedule depends on volume. Some municipalities require it by rule, so check with the Town about your obligations.

We clear the lines downstream of the trap and can jet them to remove what the trap missed. We do not pump traps ourselves. A licensed hauler does that.

Backflow prevention

The Hudson DPW runs a cross-connection control program. Commercial and irrigation connections generally need a backflow preventer, and the device must be tested on a regular schedule by a certified tester. A failed test means the device is repaired or replaced and tested again.

Ask the DPW what your account requires. We can help with the plumbing side of a repair or replacement and coordinate with the tester.

Working around business hours

We schedule non-emergency work early or late to avoid disrupting customers. For emergencies we respond at the time of the call and set up barriers and signs so staff and visitors stay safe.

If a shutdown is required, we tell you how long it will last and which areas will lose water.

Preventive visits for older buildings

A scheduled walk-through costs far less than an emergency at midnight. We look at shutoff valves, water heaters, floor drains, the main cleanout, mop sink traps and any backflow device. We note what is stuck, leaking or past its life and give you a list ranked by urgency.

Businesses that rent a space should ask the landlord which plumbing is theirs and which is the tenant's. Clear ownership of the main line, the water heater and the restrooms prevents arguments when something fails.

Commercial rules that apply in Hudson

Hudson has a grease interceptor regulation, No. 08-2, that covers food service facilities. It says new and expanded facilities need an interceptor, requires monthly inspection and cleaning before grease passes 25 percent of capacity and requires written logs kept for at least two years. It also requires waste from garbage grinders to pass through a solids interceptor first.

Backflow prevention is handled through the Town's cross-connection program, and the plumbing itself must meet the standard in the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00. We work to those documents, and we tell you when a task belongs to a licensed hauler or the DPW.
