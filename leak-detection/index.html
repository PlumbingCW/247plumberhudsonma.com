Emergency Leak Detection in Hudson, MA

The meter keeps turning with every faucet off, a ceiling stain is spreading, or the water bill doubled. We find where the water is escaping before anything gets opened up.

Active leak: stop the water first

Leak detection in Hudson, MA is a diagnostic job, so the first goal is to limit damage. If water is running through a ceiling or you hear it inside a wall, treat it as an emergency. Our dispatch line is staffed 24/7 and we offer same-day appointments when available.

  • Close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter or where the pipe enters the basement.
  • Open a low faucet to drain the lines, and switch off power to any wet room from the breaker.
  • Put a bucket under drips and move valuables. Do not cut into a wet ceiling unless water is pooling.
  • Photograph everything for your insurer before you clean up.

The meter is still running or the bill jumped

This is the most common way people find out. Shut off every tap, the ice maker and the washing machine, then watch the leak indicator on your water meter. If it keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and a fixture.

Hudson's DPW Water Division reads meters three times a year for the water and sewer bills, so a hidden leak can run for months before a bill shows it. The Town is responsible for the public main, and you are responsible for the service pipe from the street to your house. A leak on your side of the street is yours to fix.

We separate the house from the service line by closing the main valve. If the meter stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps running, it is in the buried line, which we cover on the water service line repair page.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks

A brown ring on the ceiling is old water. A dripping one is new. We find the source above it, which is often a bathroom supply line, a tub drain or a roof leak that tracks down a stud.

Where possible we open a small access hole, not a whole wall. If the leak is from a roof or a window, we tell you so and you can call a roofer instead of paying for plumbing work you do not need.

Under the house, in the crawlspace and in the yard

Older Hudson homes with a basement or a crawlspace give us a view of the pipes. We look for wet insulation, mineral streaks and corroded joints. Copper pipe in older houses can develop pinholes, and galvanized steel corrodes from the inside.

Outside, a wet patch in the lawn that stays green in a dry week points to a buried line or a leaking irrigation zone. A drip at the curb stop box or the meter pit gets a quick check, since that fitting is a common leak point. Irrigation systems are also a leak source, and backflow devices on them are inspected under the Town's program.

If your property has a pool or spa with a suspected leak, tell dispatch. We handle the plumbing side, which is the lines and fittings, and not the pool shell.

When the leak is in a drain line

Not every leak is a supply line. A drain pipe that leaks only when a fixture drains can wet a ceiling for weeks. A camera and a dye test narrow it down fast. Leaks in drain lines often show up after a long clog, and the drain cleaning page explains how the two problems connect.

What the rules say about repairing a found leak

Finding a leak is diagnostic work. The repair that follows may be exempt from permitting if it is a faucet or valve leak, according to 248 CMR 3.05, but replacing a section of pipe generally needs a permit and an inspection.

A repaired supply line must meet the standard in the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00. We tell you which category your repair falls into before we start.
