What to do before we arrive: clogs that cannot wait

Hydro jetting in Hudson, MA is used for blocked main lines and for lines that clog again and again. Dispatch is open 24/7. If a main is backing up into the house, call first and describe the symptoms. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

  • Stop running water until the plumber arrives.
  • Tell dispatch if a snake already failed or got stuck. That is a common reason to jet.
  • Clear the area around the cleanout so the hose can be set up.
  • Do not try chemical cleaners. They can splash on the technician.

The symptom people describe

Most people do not call asking for jetting. They say a plumber snaked the line and it clogged again a few weeks later, or that the snake stopped partway and could not go further. Those are the signs that the pipe wall is coated, not just plugged.

A snake cuts a path through the middle of the clog. Grease, scale and fine roots remain on the walls. Jetting removes them.

How hydro jetting works

A hose with a special nozzle feeds high-pressure water into the line. Jets fire backward and pull the nozzle forward while scouring the wall. The debris is flushed to the street connection or out through the cleanout.

We run a camera before and after. The first look shows whether the pipe can take jetting. The second look proves the line is clean.

Roots and grease in older Hudson lines

Older clay and cast iron laterals in Hudson are the usual jetting candidates. Cast iron develops a rough scale inside that traps debris. Clay joints let in fine root hairs.

Restaurants and commercial kitchens in town produce grease. A grease trap that is not pumped overflows into the line, and jetting helps clear what the trap leaves behind. See commercial plumbing for those accounts.

When jetting is a bad idea

Jetting a cracked or collapsed pipe can worsen the damage and push debris out of the break. That is why the camera comes first. If the pipe is damaged, we recommend repair or lining instead, covered under sewer line repair.

We also avoid jetting very old Orangeburg pipe, which can come apart under pressure.

What to expect on the day

The technician looks at your cleanout, runs the camera to see what the pipe can take, then sets up the jetter outside or in the basement. A hose goes in, the nozzle works its way down and the flow of water and debris is watched at the cleanout. Expect noise and some wet floor protection.

After the pass, we run the camera again and show you the clean wall. If the picture shows a crack or a root mass at a joint, we say so and explain what happens next. Jetting clears the pipe, but it does not seal a broken joint, so roots will find their way back to a defect that remains.

Plan for the water to be usable again within the hour in most cases. Run a few fixtures after we leave to confirm that the line is draining freely.

Safety and standards for jetting

Jetting uses very high water pressure, which is why a camera look comes first. Pipe condition matters because a damaged line should be repaired to the standard in the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00 and not jetted.

In Hudson, a connection to the public sewer must follow the Town's sewer rules, which also say only sewage may be discharged to the sewer. Jetting through your cleanout keeps the debris out of the Town system.
