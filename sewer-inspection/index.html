Sewer Camera Inspection in Hudson, MA

A small camera on a flexible cable travels the whole line and shows what is wrong. It turns a guess into a picture, and it can save you from digging in the wrong place.

Call (508) 389-4045

What a sewer camera inspection is

A sewer camera inspection in Hudson, MA sends a waterproof camera from a cleanout, or through a pulled toilet, down the lateral to the street. The technician watches the live picture, records it and marks the location of every problem with a locator.

It is a planned service, not an emergency one. If sewage is backing up right now, start with sewer line cleaning and add the camera once the line is open.

When you should book one

Book a camera inspection if a drain has clogged more than once, if you are buying a house with an older lateral, or if you are about to put a deck or driveway over the line. A pre-purchase look can reveal a collapsed section before the closing.

Hudson has many homes built in the shoe-factory and mid-century eras, and some have their original clay or cast iron laterals. Those are the lines that benefit most from a look. Homes near mature street trees are at risk for root entry.

What you receive

You get the video and a plain description of what we saw. We mark the depth and the surface location of each defect with paint or flags, then explain the options in order of cost. You decide what, if anything, to repair.

A short inspection of a clear line takes under an hour. Heavy scale or a long run takes longer. If the line is blocked, we clear it first so the camera can pass.

Inspections for buyers, sellers and landlords

A camera report is useful in a sale. Buyers use it to negotiate and sellers use it to show the line is sound. Landlords use it to decide whether to line or replace a lateral before tenants complain.

If you are on a septic system instead of town sewer, the line from the house to the tank is the one we inspect, and the tank itself needs a septic professional. Septic systems in Massachusetts fall under the Title 5 rules.

Cleanouts and access

Most modern houses have a cleanout, a capped pipe near the foundation or in the basement. Older houses may not. If there is no cleanout, we go through a toilet, which means pulling it and resetting it with a new wax ring.

We recommend adding a cleanout while a line is open. It costs little and every future visit is quicker.

What the camera does not replace

A camera inspection is a diagnostic service, so it is not itself permit work. Any repair that follows must meet the standard in the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00 and may need a permit under 248 CMR 3.05.

If your home is on septic, the tank and leach field fall under Title 5, 310 CMR 15.00, which has its own inspection rules.
