Whole-House Repiping in Hudson, MA

When the same pipes keep leaking, patching costs more over time than replacing them. We plan repiping around your walls, your schedule and your budget.

Call (508) 389-4045

When repiping makes sense

Repiping in Hudson, MA is worth considering when you have repeated leaks, discolored water, low pressure throughout the house or old galvanized pipe. One leak does not mean a house needs repiping. Three leaks in two years often does.

If a pipe is leaking now, start with burst pipe repair to stop the damage, then decide on the larger job.

Signs the plumbing is near the end

These patterns point toward replacement.

  • Pinhole leaks in copper that appear in more than one place.
  • Brown or orange water when the tap first opens.
  • Low pressure at every fixture, not just one.
  • Old galvanized steel pipe, which rusts from the inside.
  • Visible green corrosion at fittings or flaking on the outside of pipes.

What material to use

Two materials dominate modern repiping.

PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, fits through walls, resists freeze splitting better than rigid pipeNot for outdoor sun exposure, needs proper support.

How a repipe goes

We walk the house and map the existing lines, then give you a written scope. We open small access holes in walls and ceilings, run new lines and make the final connections in a planned shutdown. Most whole-house repipes take several days.

We keep the house in use when we can by restoring water at the end of each day. Drywall patches are included in the plan, and painting is not, so ask us to explain the finishes.

Partial repiping

Not every house needs everything replaced. A bad bathroom stack, a basement run or the water service line can be done alone. A partial job costs less and fixes the weak point. The tradeoff is that old pipe elsewhere may fail later.

If the trouble starts at the street, see water service line repair.

Permits and inspection

Repiping needs a plumbing permit through the Hudson Building Department at 78 Main Street, and the work is inspected. A permit protects you when you sell the house and ensures the new piping meets Massachusetts plumbing code.

We handle the permit and schedule the inspection.

Preparing your house for a repipe

Clear the areas near walls we will open, including closets, under-sink cabinets and the basement run. Move furniture away from the wall and take art down. We lay protection on floors and keep dust contained as much as we can.

Plan for short periods without water. Fill a few jugs for drinking and cooking, and decide in advance where you will wash. If anyone in the home depends on water for medical equipment, tell us on the first visit so we can plan around it.

We label every shutoff when we finish and show you where each one is. It is a lasting benefit of a repipe, since a house that has a shutoff for every fixture makes later repairs quick.

Insurance usually does not pay to replace pipe that has simply worn out. It may help with water damage from a sudden failure. Ask your agent before work begins if a claim is open, because some insurers want to inspect the damage first.

Water quality after a repipe

New pipe can release fine debris or a slight taste for a few days. We flush every line and clean the fixture aerators before we leave. Run the taps for a minute or two in the morning during the first week.

If you see persistent brown or cloudy water after the flush, call us. It can mean a disturbed deposit in the service line or the water heater, and it is worth checking.

Codes and inspections for repiping

Replacement piping must meet the standard in the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, including approved materials, support spacing and testing. The state regulation, 248 CMR 3.05, requires a permit before work starts and an inspection before walls are closed.

We keep the permit card on site and tell you when the inspection is scheduled.
