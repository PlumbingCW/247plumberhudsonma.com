Water heaters: repair, replacement and planning

A water heater is the appliance people notice only when it stops. Water heater repair is the page for a tank that makes noise, leaks a little or has lost its hot water. It explains the tests we run and when a repair is worth the money.

When the tank itself has failed, water heater replacement covers the swap, including haul-away of the old unit. If you would rather plan ahead, water heater installation walks through sizing, venting and permits.

On-demand units have their own quirks, from error codes to scale on the heat exchanger. Tankless water heater service covers installs, flushes and repairs.

Drains and sewers: from one sink to the main line

Start with drain cleaning when one sink, tub or floor drain is slow or stopped. If every fixture is affected, the blockage is probably in the main, and sewer line cleaning is the right page.

Lines that clog again soon after snaking usually have grease, scale or roots on the pipe wall. Hydro jetting cleans that wall, and a sewer camera inspection shows what is inside before you spend more.

When the camera finds a break, sewer line repair compares spot repair, lining, bursting and replacement, with the trade-off of each in plain terms.

Leaks and supply pipes

Spraying water is a burst pipe repair call. The page lists what to do in the first minutes and how we find and fix the break.

A stain with no visible source, or a bill that climbs for no reason, calls for leak detection, which uses listening and thermal tools to find the leak before anything is opened. The pipe that runs from the street to your meter has its own page, water service line repair.

Pipes that keep failing in several places may be at the end of their life. Repiping explains whole-house and partial replacement and how to choose a material.

Fixtures, disposals and new work

Small parts cause many calls. Toilet repair handles overflows, running tanks and leaks at the base, and faucet and fixture repair covers drips, shower valves and stuck shutoffs.

Garbage disposal repair covers jams and leaks as well as new installs. For bigger projects, plumbing installation covers bathrooms, kitchens and laundry hookups, with the permit handled for you.

Basements and businesses

A basement that takes on water in a storm needs a working pump. Sump pump repair covers failed pumps, check valves and battery backups, and the rules on where the water may go.

Restaurants, shops and offices have their own needs, from grease lines to backflow-related repairs. Commercial plumbing explains how we work around business hours.

Repair or replace: a rule of thumb

Plumbing decisions often come down to whether to fix a part or replace the whole thing. A leaking faucet cartridge or a worn toilet flapper is nearly always worth repairing, because the part is cheap and the fixture is fine. A water heater that has begun to leak from the tank is the opposite case, since the tank cannot be patched.

Pipes sit in between. One pinhole leak in an otherwise sound line is a repair. Several leaks in a few years, rust-colored water and low pressure in every room point toward replacement, and the repiping page explains how to weigh it. Dispatch can talk it through if you call (508) 389-4045.

For drains, the question is whether the pipe is sound. A camera answers it, which is why we suggest a look before an expensive repair, and why the inspection page exists.

Permits and licensed work

According to the Massachusetts plumbing and gas code, 248 CMR, some work needs a permit. Replacing a water heater, running new drains and altering gas piping are examples. Repairing a leaking faucet or clearing a stoppage is not. A licensed plumber pulls the permit through the Hudson Building Department at 78 Main Street, and the inspector checks the work before it is closed up.

If a quote does not mention a permit for work that needs one, ask why. We list the permit step on the pages where it applies, such as water heater installation and plumbing installation.
