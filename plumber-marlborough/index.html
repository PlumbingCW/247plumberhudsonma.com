Marlborough plumbing emergencies we are called for

Our emergency plumber service for Marlborough, MA starts with a phone call that is answered around the clock. Marlborough is the closest large market to Hudson, about four miles to the south, and dispatch plans routes to it with I-495 and the local roads in mind. We offer same-day appointments when available.

The calls we hear most often from Marlborough addresses are drains that back up at the lowest fixture, water heaters that fail without warning, and supply lines that burst in cold snaps.

  • Sewage or gray water coming up in a basement floor drain or tub.
  • A water heater leaking at the base or producing no hot water.
  • A burst or frozen pipe spraying inside a wall, ceiling or crawlspace.
  • A toilet that overflows and will not stop.

Before we arrive

A few minutes of preparation limit damage while the technician is on the way.

Shut off the water

Find the main valve where the line enters the house and turn it clockwise. If you cannot find it, close the nearest fixture valve and tell dispatch.

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Never step into standing water near outlets.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone. Open a path to the water heater, the cleanout and the main shutoff.

Call and describe it

Call (508) 389-4045 and tell dispatch your Marlborough address, where the water is coming from and whether sewage is involved.

A city with older neighborhoods and newer apartments

Marlborough mixes older single-family streets near the downtown core with newer condominium and apartment complexes along the commercial corridors. Older houses are more likely to have galvanized or cast iron pipe and a clay sewer lateral that has seen decades of roots. Newer buildings are more likely to have PEX supply lines, plastic drains and shared water heaters.

That matters because the likely failure differs. In an older house we often find a belly or root intrusion in the lateral. In a newer condo, a supply stop or a water heater is more often the cause. See sewer camera inspection for the older-pipe case.

City water and sewer: where our responsibility ends

The Marlborough Water and Sewer Division maintains the city's water mains and sewer collection system. According to its page, after hours, nights, weekends and holidays, callers reach the Marlborough Police Department at 508-485-1212 for city water and sewer emergencies.

That is useful because the dividing line is the property edge. If the problem is in the street main, the division handles it. If it is in the line from the main to your house, or anywhere inside, it is yours, and that is where we work. The city also has a program on illegal sump pump and roof drain connections, so a sump pump should never drain into the sewer.

Shared history with Hudson

Fort Meadow Reservoir sits between the two communities. It is a recreation area both towns use, and Centennial Beach on the Hudson side is run by the Town of Hudson. That shared shoreline is one reason our crews know the roads on both sides.

Southborough and Hopkinton lie further to the south and southeast. We can often cover calls from those towns when the schedule allows, so ask dispatch if you live just outside Marlborough.

Cold weather and frozen pipes

Massachusetts winters bring days when pipes in exterior walls and unheated garages freeze. A pipe that has frozen but not yet split gives you time. Open the faucet, warm the wall with a hair dryer and call us. A pipe that has split will leak when it thaws, so shut the main before the temperature rises.

Learn where the main shutoff is before winter. In older Marlborough houses it is often in the basement near the meter, and an old valve may need replacing so that it will turn when you need it. See burst pipe repair.
