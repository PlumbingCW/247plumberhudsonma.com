Garbage disposal problems

Garbage disposal repair in Hudson, MA starts with the sound. A hum with no spin means a jam. Silence means a tripped reset or a failed motor. A leak means a seal or a cracked housing. Dispatch is open 24/7 if a leak is spreading, and routine disposal work is scheduled.

Never put your hand in the disposal, even when it is off. Use tongs or pliers to remove an object.

What not to put down a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds in large amounts, pasta, rice and fibrous vegetables such as celery and corn husks cause clogs. Bones and fruit pits can jam the blades. Grease cools and coats the pipe.

A disposal does not replace a trash bin. Scrape plates and run cold water for several seconds before and after use.

Repair or replace

A jam or a tripped reset is a quick repair. A leaking housing or a burnt-out motor is a replacement. Disposals have limited lives, and a unit that has needed repeated repairs is worth swapping.

We size the new unit to the household. A larger horsepower motor grinds faster and jams less.

Installing a new disposal

A new install needs a switch or a plug, a dishwasher knockout opened if the dishwasher connects, and the drain trap reconnected. If your kitchen has no disposal, we confirm the wiring and the sink can take one.

Some homes on septic systems should avoid disposals because they add solids to the tank. Ask a septic professional if you are unsure.

When the clog is not the disposal

If water backs up in the sink next to the disposal, the problem is often in the kitchen drain line. A cable machine clears it. A kitchen line that clogs again soon after may have a belly or heavy grease buildup. See hydro jetting.

If every drain in the house is slow, the main line is the issue.

How to unjam a disposal safely

Turn the wall switch off, and unplug the unit if it has a cord. Look under the sink for a hex socket in the center of the bottom plate. Insert the hex key that came with the unit, or a three-eighths inch one, and work it back and forth until it turns freely. Then press the red reset button on the base.

If the jam returns right away, something is wedged in the grinding chamber. Use tongs to pull it out, never your fingers. A spoon or a bottle cap is often the culprit.

If the hex key will not turn the shaft at all, the bearings may have seized. If the motor hums and the shaft turns by hand but will not spin under power, the motor has failed. Both are replacement jobs.

After any repair, run cold water and a handful of ice cubes through the unit to clear residue. Avoid pouring bleach or harsh drain cleaners into a disposal, since they can corrode the seals.

Rules and standards to know

Repairing a leak at a disposal or its connections is exempt from permitting under 248 CMR 3.05. A new installation involves wiring and drain work, and the electrical part follows the National Electrical Code, NFPA 70, while the drain follows the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00.

If your home is on septic, the state septic rules, Title 5 at 310 CMR 15.00, govern the system itself, and your Board of Health is the right contact about adding solids.

What to do before you touch a jammed disposal

Switch the unit off at the wall, and unplug it if it has a cord. Then switch off the breaker as well, since a switch can be bumped back on while your hand is near the blades.

Never reach into the grinding chamber. Use tongs or long pliers to lift out a spoon, a bottle cap or a fruit pit. Do not pour drain cleaner into a unit that is jammed or leaking, because it can splash and corrode the seals. If the leak is spreading under the sink, close the two valves below the sink and call.
