Installing a water heater the planned way

Water heater installation in Hudson, MA covers more than swapping one tank for another. The first job is choosing the right size and fuel for how your household uses hot water. The second is making sure the connections, venting and safety devices meet current Massachusetts plumbing and fuel gas rules.

If your heater has already failed and the basement is wet, the faster path is water heater replacement, which is staffed 24/7. This page is for scheduled installs, new construction and upgrades.

Choosing the right tank

A tank heater is sized by first-hour rating, which is how much hot water it delivers in the first hour of heavy use. A household with one bathroom and two people can usually run a 40-gallon tank. A family with several showers each morning needs more. We ask about fixtures, not just people.

Fuel type matters too. Natural gas heats faster and costs less to run where gas is available. Electric units are simpler to vent and are common where gas is not piped to the street. Heat pump water heaters use far less electricity but need space and air flow around them.

What a proper install includes

A clean install has parts that homeowners often do not see until they are missing.

  • A new drain pan and drain line where a leak would damage a finished floor.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve piped to a safe termination, never capped.
  • Shutoff valves and flexible connectors in good condition.
  • An expansion tank when the house has a closed system, such as a check valve or a pressure reducing valve.
  • Proper venting, sloped and sealed, for gas units.
  • A gas leak test on every fitting before the unit is lit.

Permits and inspection

A water heater installation requires a plumbing permit in Hudson, and a gas unit also needs a gas permit. A licensed plumber pulls both through the Building Department at 78 Main Street. A permit lets the Town inspect the venting and gas connections, which is the part that protects your family from carbon monoxide.

Install a carbon monoxide alarm near the bedrooms if you do not already have one. Massachusetts requires them in homes with fuel-burning appliances.

Old tank removal and what happens next

We drain and disconnect the old tank and haul it away. Many scrap yards take them. We also check the water line shutoff and the gas valve, since an old valve that will not close is the reason a job can stall.

Plan for the house to be without hot water for several hours. A typical swap takes half a day, longer if the venting or the electrical supply needs work.

Sizing and efficiency in practice

A larger tank is not always better. It costs more to buy and loses more heat while it sits. We match the unit to your peak demand, and for households that use hot water at predictable times, a smaller tank with a quick recovery rate often works well.

The efficiency rating on the label tells you how much of the fuel becomes hot water. Higher efficiency costs more up front, and the savings depend on how much hot water you use. We show you both numbers so you can decide.

Standards and references

The Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00, sets the plumbing standard for water heater connections, and the Massachusetts Fuel Gas Code, 248 CMR 4.00, sets it for gas supply and venting. Permits and inspections follow 248 CMR 3.05.

If your house is a candidate for a heat pump water heater, ask Hudson Light and Power about rebates before you buy.
