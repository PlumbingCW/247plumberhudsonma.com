Main line blocked: act now

Sewer line cleaning in Hudson, MA is needed when the main drain between your house and the street stops carrying wastewater. Dispatch is available 24/7 and we offer same-day appointments when available. A blocked main usually gives warning signs first.

  • Several fixtures drain slowly or gurgle at the same time.
  • Water comes up in the shower or the lowest floor drain when you flush or run the washer.
  • A sewage smell appears in the basement or yard.
  • The cleanout cap outside is wet or the pipe is full.

How a blocked main differs from a clogged drain

A fixture drain clog affects one sink or tub. A main line clog affects the whole house, because every branch drains into it. The tools are heavier, the cleanout is the access point and the cause is more often roots or a long grease buildup.

If only one fixture is affected, start with drain cleaning. If several are, you are in the right place.

How we clear the main

We find the cleanout and run a cable machine with a cutting head through the line. For roots, we use a root cutter. For grease, hot water jetting is more effective. We then run a camera to confirm the line is open and to see why it clogged.

A cleaned line is not a repaired line. If the camera shows a broken section, you need a repair, and we explain it on the sewer line repair page.

Roots, grease and wipes

Roots are the main cause in older lines. They grow through joints seeking water, and they come back unless the joint is repaired or lined. Grease cools in the pipe and builds up on the walls.

Wipes labeled as flushable do not break down. The Town of Hudson DPW says only sewage belongs in the sewer. Grease and wipes that reach a pump station can stop it, which is why the Town asks residents to keep them out.

Storm drains and yard drains

Not all yard drains are sewer drains. A driveway or foundation drain that takes rainwater is a storm drain and is kept separate. Sump pumps may not discharge to the sanitary sewer in Hudson. If a yard drain backs up after a storm, we can clean it and check where it leads.

Leaves, silt and pine needles are the usual cause. We cable the line and flush it, and we recommend a screen at the inlet.

Keeping the main clear

Do not pour grease down the drain. Do not flush wipes, paper towels or hygiene products. Cut back plants that grow close to the lateral, and schedule a camera look every few years if you have an older clay line.

A main that is jetted once and again after a few years stays clearer than one cleared only in emergencies. See hydro jetting for the full-wash option.
