If the water heater is leaking, do this first

Water heater repair in Hudson, MA starts with safety. A tank that is dripping can be a loose fitting, but a tank that is pooling water at the base has usually failed inside. We answer 24/7 and can often book a same-day visit, so call at any hour.

  • Close the cold water shutoff on the pipe above the tank. Turn the handle until it stops.
  • Switch the unit off. For electric heaters, flip the breaker. For gas, set the control to pilot or off.
  • If the leak is heavy, open a hot tap upstairs to relieve pressure, and keep people and electronics away from wet floors.
  • Smell gas? Leave the house and call 911 and your gas utility first, then call us.

Gas, electric, hybrid and tankless units

Most Hudson homes run a gas or electric tank. Hudson's electricity comes from the municipal Hudson Light and Power Department, and its rates are one factor when you weigh gas, electric and heat pump models. Hybrid heat pump water heaters have a compressor on top and need open air around them, so the repair is different from a plain electric tank.

Tankless units fail in their own ways, usually scale on the heat exchanger or an error code from a clogged inlet filter. A flush often fixes it, and we cover that on the tankless water heater page.

We also check the venting and the combustion air on gas units. A backdrafting flue is a carbon monoxide risk and is fixed before anything else.

Why Hudson tanks fail early

Hudson's drinking water blends surface water from the Gates Pond Reservoir with groundwater from town wells, which the Town treats to remove iron and manganese. Water with dissolved minerals leaves scale in a tank over time, and a tank in a hard-working household can fill with sediment sooner than the label suggests.

The practical answer is cheap. Drain a few gallons from the valve at the bottom once a year, and check the anode rod every few years. A tank that is ten to fifteen years old and starting to leak is not worth a repair, and we will say so.

Cold-weather and basement problems

Many local water heaters sit in an unfinished basement or a garage. A leak there can run for days before anyone sees it. If the floor around the tank is damp, check it before you assume the tank is fine.

Water heaters in unheated spaces can also freeze a supply line during a New England cold snap. If one has burst, see our burst pipe repair page and shut off the main first.

Rules for gas and electric heaters

Repairs to a gas water heater fall under the Massachusetts Fuel Gas Code, 248 CMR 4.00, which builds on NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, with Massachusetts amendments. A licensed plumber or gas fitter should handle gas connections, vent repairs and controls.

Relief valve repairs also have a standard to meet. Relief valve piping follows the Massachusetts Uniform State Plumbing Code, 248 CMR 10.00 and must end where a discharge cannot scald anyone. A valve that is dripping is doing its job or has failed, and either way it needs attention.
